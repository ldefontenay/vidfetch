What it does

VidFetch is an accessible interface for YouTube, made for screen reader users first. You can search YouTube, listen without downloading, follow channels without an account, go through a channel's videos and playlists, and read descriptions. It can also download from YouTube and from many other video sites, videos and whole playlists, as sound only to begin with, or as video if you choose. Each download is saved in the output folder, named after its title. A playlist gets a folder of its own, and fetching it again later brings only its new videos.

Downloading a video or a playlist

  1. In the browser, open the video or the playlist, and copy its address with Alt+D then Ctrl+C.
  2. In VidFetch, press Ctrl+N, New download. The address on the clipboard is already in the web address box, chosen, so pasting or typing another replaces it.
  3. Press Enter to start. Tab first if you want to check how it will be saved: the next two boxes are Download as (Alt+A) and Picture quality (Alt+Q), changed with the arrow keys, and the What will happen box (Alt+H) says what this address is and where the download goes.

A YouTube video that is part of a playlist, such as one opened from the playlist's page, has both in its address. VidFetch then asks whether to download the whole playlist or just this video; Enter, or Escape, downloads just the video. A YouTube mix, the endless list YouTube makes for a video, is never taken as a playlist.

The Status box says how far the download has got. Escape stops it. When it is done, VidFetch says so, and the download is chosen in the list: Enter plays it.

If you ask for a video you already have, at the same format and quality, VidFetch asks whether to download it again; Enter, or Escape, cancels, and the copy you have is chosen in the list. A different format or picture quality just downloads. VidFetch remembers a download if you rename it in VidFetch, but not if you rename or move it in File Explorer.

The queue

While one download runs, press Ctrl+N for the next, or Ctrl+U on a playlist: it waits its turn, with the choices it had when you asked for it. The downloads that follow start by themselves, without a word; VidFetch says when each one finishes, and the Status box says how far the current one has got and how many more are waiting.

Escape stops only the download running now, and the next one starts. Show queue (Ctrl+Q) lists what is running and what is waiting; Delete takes the chosen one out, and Remove all empties the queue. So to stop everything, press Ctrl+Q, remove all, close it, and press Escape.

The queue shows each download's title, once it is known: a few seconds after it is added, or longer for a playlist or another site. Until then it shows the address.

If a download fails, the others carry on, and VidFetch says why once they are all done.

The queue is kept when VidFetch closes, with the download that was running. VidFetch asks before exiting while something runs or waits, and the next time it starts, it lists what had not finished and asks whether to carry on downloading or empty the queue. A download that was stopped half way starts again from the beginning; a playlist carries on with the videos it does not have yet. Empty queue, in the Download menu, takes out everything waiting at any time, after asking.

Playlists and channels

A playlist's videos are downloaded one after the other into a folder named after the playlist. Their files start with their place in the playlist, such as 03 - Title.mp4, so they sort in its order; Number the files of a playlist, in the Options menu, turns that off. A channel's address, such as its Videos page, works the same way.

A video that cannot be downloaded, because it is private or was removed, does not stop the rest. The report at the end lists each one with the reason.

Stopping keeps every video already saved. To carry on, or to fetch videos added to the playlist since, choose the playlist in the list and press Ctrl+U, Update playlist: only the videos not yet in its folder are downloaded. The folder holds a small file, VidFetch.json, which records which videos are there. You can rename the folder as you like, in VidFetch or in File Explorer, but keep that file in it.

Playing

Enter on a download plays it in VidFetch's own Play window, which opens at once. Its title says what is playing. It carries on from where you stopped last time, and says so; stopping within 10 seconds of the start or the end is not remembered, so a finished one starts again.

  • Play or pause (Alt+P, or Enter or the Space bar on it).
  • Back 10 seconds (Alt+B) and Forward 10 seconds (Alt+F).
  • The Left and Right arrows: 2 seconds back or forward. Home: the start. End: 10 seconds before the end. T: where playing is, said out loud, with the chapter.
  • The Up and Down arrows: louder or quieter, by 5%.
  • Shift with Up and Down: faster or slower, a quarter at a time, from half speed to three times as fast, with voices kept at their own pitch. The speed is kept for the next one, and is also in the Options menu.
  • Page Down and Page Up: the next or the previous in the list, saying only its name. With Continuous play ticked in the Options menu, the next starts by itself. From YouTube, the next one's sound is found while this one plays, so it starts at once.
  • Ctrl+Page Down and Ctrl+Page Up: the next chapter, or back to the start of this one, of a YouTube video whose description lists its chapters. Each chapter's name is said.
  • Ctrl+D, while listening to a video online: downloads it.
  • Where playing is (Alt+W): the time, such as "1:23 of 15:04, playing", to read with the arrow keys. It changes as it plays, even while it has focus.
  • Escape stops and closes it.

Cut silences when playing, in the Options menu, is off to begin with. When it is on, every silence is cut down to a second; where playing is still counts the time cut.

The Play window plays the sound only, of a video too. To see a video's picture, choose Open video using default media player in its context menu or the Download menu, which plays it in the program Windows plays it with.

What the keys do is also in the Keys box (Alt+K) of the Play window.

A few YouTube videos cannot be streamed, because YouTube's sound file for them carries a note that stops Windows reading it as it arrives. VidFetch finds this out when no sound comes, says so, fetches the whole of the sound first, and then plays it. That takes a few seconds longer than usual, the first time on any day; everything else works as usual. A download of such a video is not affected.

Listening to an address

In New download (Ctrl+N), Listen without downloading (Alt+L) plays the sound of the video whose address you gave, from YouTube or another site, in the Play window. Ctrl+D there downloads it after all.

The YouTube window

What comes from YouTube has a window of its own, in front of the main one, which waits behind it. Downloads carry on meanwhile. It opens with Search YouTube (Ctrl+F), New videos from channels (Ctrl+L), Your channels (Ctrl+Shift+L) or Recently played (Ctrl+H), in the main window's YouTube menu or in its own Go menu (Alt+G). Its title and its list's name say what it shows.

  • Enter listens to a video straight from YouTube in the Play window, without downloading it. Closing the Play window leaves you on the same video, and Enter carries on from there. On a channel or a playlist, Enter opens it; the Enter button says which.
  • Ctrl+D downloads a video with your usual settings, into the category shown in the main window. On a playlist or a channel, it downloads them all, after asking.
  • Ctrl+I shows a video's description.
  • The Video menu (Alt+V) and the context menu (Shift+F10) also have Show channel, Show channel's playlists, Follow and Unfollow channel, Copy web address and Open in browser.
  • Escape or Backspace goes back one list at a time, as in File Explorer. From the first list, Escape closes the window; so does Close window (Alt+C).

Searching asks YouTube directly, which is quick; if that ever fails, it searches with yt-dlp instead, which is slower. The last line, More, fetches the next page when you press Enter on it.

Following channels

VidFetch keeps its own list of channels you follow, with no account and no signing in. To follow a channel:

  • Follow its channel, in the context menu of a video found, or of a download.
  • Paste a channel's address in New download (Ctrl+N), and press Follow the channel instead (Alt+F).

Ctrl+L, New videos from channels, fetches the latest videos of every channel you follow and lists them, newest first. Those that were not there when you last pressed Ctrl+L start with "New". Channels are only checked when you ask.

Ctrl+Shift+L, Your channels, lists the channels you follow. Enter on one shows its latest videos; Delete stops following it, after asking.

The last line of a channel's videos is its playlists. Enter lists them, each with how many videos it has; Enter on one shows its videos in its order, and Ctrl+D downloads it whole, after asking.

Recently played

Ctrl+H lists, in the YouTube window, the last hundred things you played, downloads and videos online alike, newest first, each with where you stopped. Enter carries on from there; Delete takes one off the list, and deletes nothing. Enter on a download whose file has gone offers to download it again, if it came from YouTube.

Categories

Categories are folders inside the output folder, so File Explorer shows the same as VidFetch. Downloads that are in no category are in a folder called Uncategorised. The first time VidFetch starts with categories, it moves the downloads already in the output folder into Uncategorised, and says so.

  • Category (Alt+C), above the list: one category, or All categories. New downloads go into the category shown, or into Uncategorised when all are shown. New download has its own Category box too.
  • The Category menu has New category, Rename category, and Delete category, which act on the category shown. Deleting a category moves what is in it into Uncategorised; nothing is lost.
  • Ctrl+Shift+M, Move to category, moves the download chosen, a playlist whole, into another category, or a new one.

A playlist's videos

Enter on a playlist shows its videos in the list, in the playlist's order, in place of all downloads; the list's name says which playlist it is. Everything works on them as on any download: Enter plays, F2 renames, Delete deletes, and the context menu has the rest. Escape, or Backspace, goes back to all downloads, on that playlist. While the list shows a playlist's videos, Escape goes back rather than stopping a download; press it again to stop.

A video deleted from a playlist's folder is not downloaded again when you update the playlist.

Video or sound only

Download as, in the Options menu and in the New download window:

  • Sound only, M4A, the choice to begin with: the sound exactly as the site sends it, nothing lost and nothing converted. Most players, phones and Windows itself play it.
  • Sound only, MP3: for a player that takes nothing else, such as an older car stereo or a talking book player. It is converted, so it takes a little longer.
  • Video: an MP4 with picture and sound, which every player plays. YouTube sends the picture and the sound separately; VidFetch puts them together with Windows' own Media Foundation, copying both as they are, so nothing is lost.

Picture quality is how many lines high the picture is at most: 360, 480, 720 or 1080. Higher is sharper and larger. YouTube offers the kind of picture an MP4 holds up to 1080 lines; a video that is only in higher quality comes down at the best it has up to that.

Each file is tagged with its title, the channel that put it online, the year, and the address it came from; a playlist's videos also with the playlist's name as the album and their place as the track number. Media players and File Explorer show these.

Captions

Save captions, in the Options menu and the New download window, is off to begin with. When it is on, each download's captions are saved beside it as an SRT file, which media players show, such as Title.en.srt for Title.mp4. A person's captions are chosen when there are some in the video's language or in English, otherwise the site's automatic ones, in the language spoken. When a video has none, or the site refuses them, VidFetch says so, and the download itself is kept. Renaming or deleting a download in VidFetch does the same to its captions.

Show captions, in the Download menu and the context menu, shows a download's saved captions as running text, in paragraphs, each starting with its time, such as [1:23], to read with the arrow keys.

In the captions, a description, or any other message, Copy all text (Alt+O) copies all of it. To copy a part, select it and press Ctrl+C.

The main window

The list of downloads, most recent first, shows each file and playlist in the output folder, with what it is and when it was saved. Tab moves from the list to the buttons, then to the Status box:

  • Category (Alt+C): which category the list shows, or all of them.
  • Downloads (Alt+W): the list. Enter plays the chosen download, or shows a playlist's videos, F2 renames it, Delete deletes it, and Shift+F10 or the Applications key opens its context menu.
  • Play (Alt+P or Enter): plays the download in the Play window. On a playlist, it is called Open playlist.
  • New download (Alt+N or Ctrl+N).
  • Update playlist (Alt+U or Ctrl+U): fetches the chosen playlist's videos that are not in its folder yet.
  • Show queue (Alt+Q or Ctrl+Q): what is running and what is waiting.
  • Rename (Alt+R or F2): a new name for the file, keeping its ending, or for the playlist's folder.
  • Show video in file explorer (Alt+X): opens the output folder with the download chosen in it.
  • Delete (Alt+L or Delete in the list): asks, with "Keep it" the default, then moves the file, or the playlist's folder, to the Recycle Bin.
  • Status (Alt+T): what is happening now. Pressing Alt+T again says it again.

The menus

A menu item that cannot apply now is greyed out, such as Update playlist on a single video. The buttons are never greyed out: they say why instead.

  • File: New download; Select output folder; Open library in file explorer; Update yt-dlp; Exit.
  • Download: what can be done to the download chosen in the list: Open playlist or play video; Open video using default media player; Back; Update playlist; Show description (Ctrl+I); Show captions; Show queue (Ctrl+Q); Empty queue; Stop download (Escape); Rename; Move to category; Show video in file explorer; Delete.
  • YouTube: Search YouTube (Ctrl+F); New videos from channels (Ctrl+L); Your channels (Ctrl+Shift+L); Recently played (Ctrl+H); and, for a download from YouTube, Follow channel, Unfollow channel, Copy web address and Open in browser.
  • Category: New category, Rename category and Delete category; Move to category (Ctrl+Shift+M).
  • Options: Download as; Picture quality; Number playlist files in order; Save captions file; Playing speed; Continuous play; Cut silences when playing; Offer copied web addresses, which, when you copy a web address elsewhere and come back to VidFetch, opens New download with it, where you can download it or listen to it. Each choice is saved at once and said.
  • Help: this page (F1), and About VidFetch.

yt-dlp

VidFetch downloads with yt-dlp, a free program that knows YouTube and well over a thousand other sites. The first time, VidFetch asks to download it from its official page on GitHub, checks it against its published checksum, and keeps it in its own folder, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\VidFetch\tools. If yt-dlp is already on the computer's path, that one is used instead.

Video sites change often, and an old yt-dlp is the usual reason a download fails. VidFetch updates its copy every two weeks by itself; Update yt-dlp, in the File menu, does it now.

What VidFetch cannot download

  • Private, age-restricted or members-only videos, which need signing in.
  • Videos a site offers only as a stream in pieces, which would need FFmpeg to put together. VidFetch says so when that is the reason.
  • Live streams while they are live.

Only download what you have the right to: your own videos, freely licensed ones, or for your own use where the law and the site allow it.

Where things are kept

  • Downloads: Videos\VidFetch in your user folder, unless you choose another in the File menu.
  • Settings: %APPDATA%\VidFetch\settings.txt, plain text, safe to edit while VidFetch is closed. Beside it, downloaded.json remembers which single videos you have, to notice one asked for again, and queue.json holds what had not finished when VidFetch closed; channels.json the channels you follow, places.json where you stopped playing each one, and history.json what you played lately.
  • yt-dlp, and downloads in progress: %LOCALAPPDATA%\VidFetch. A stopped download is kept there for a day, so starting it again carries on where it was.
